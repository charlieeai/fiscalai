Where Nike has a strong presence

  • Basketball: half or more of the All-NBA in every cut, with SGA, Wembanyama, Dončić and Cunningham in signature shoes.
  • Golf: has the OWGR number 1 in every cut since 2022 (Scheffler or McIlroy), and both as 1 and 2 in 2026.
  • Tennis, at the top: Sinner and Alcaraz, the two best players on tour.
  • Prospects: {{prom:nike}} of {{prom:total}} athletes aged 23 or under in 2025–2026 ({{promlist:nike}}).

Where it is weak or falling

  • Football: went from {{futbol.2023}} in 2023 to {{futbol.2025}} in 2025; Adidas has 6 of the 2025 top 10 and the youngest prospects (Yamal and Cubarsí, 19). Mbappé, Kane and Lewandowski (4F, 2021) left.
  • NFL: from {{nfl.2025}} in 2025 to {{nfl.2026}} in 2026 after losing Josh Allen (New Balance) and McCaffrey (While On Earth); Burrow has worn Alo since 2024 and Stafford, johnnie-O since Mar 2026.
  • Athletics: none of the 2025 Athlete of the Year finalists has a Nike contract ({{atletismo.2025}}): Duplantis is Puma; Furlani and the distance runners, Adidas.
  • Tennis, in depth: beyond Sinner and Alcaraz, it lost Fritz, Rublev, Tiafoe, Draper, Musetti and Raducanu.
  • Baseball: low presence, held up by Jordan Brand (Judge and Betts).

Who gains what Nike loses: Adidas in football; On (with equity as part of the pay) in tennis and with Mbappé and El Bakkali; new or own brands in the NFL and basketball (New Balance, While On Earth, 741, 361).

What the 10-K says: Nike is not cutting what it commits to athletes. Endorsement obligations payable within 12 months have held at US$1.6–1.7B since FY2024, and the FY2026 10-K lists the growing influence of athletes' personal brands as a risk.

Open question: these data cannot tell whether Nike is reallocating money to fewer names or losing bidding contests; there are cases of both (Yamal and, according to The Athletic, Mbappé chose another brand; Harden, Federer and Rublev left because Nike did not match or did not renew). On Mbappé the sources contradict each other.
