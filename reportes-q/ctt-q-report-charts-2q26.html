Read of the quarter: beat on the top line and volumes, miss on profit and guidance. The core (Express & Parcels + Mail) grew revenue and profit, but Cacesa (customs clearing) collapsed on regulatory change (Spain's G4 transit rules + the EU ending the €150 de-minimis exemption), cutting non-CEP EBIT from €5.7M to €0.6M and forcing the FY26 EBIT guide down to €115–125M. Offsetting: CEP volumes re-accelerated to +20.7%, leverage fell to 1.8×, the buyback was topped up (+€10M) and ramped below €6, and Banco CTT received an unsolicited, non-binding approach (Grupo Cajamar, 21 Jul). Update: those talks ended without agreement on 4 Sep (ECO) and no price was published; a sale now needs a new buyer and a new process. FiscalAI does not carry analyst estimates; beat/miss is vs. the reported prior-year period and guidance.

Revenue (€M, bars) & YoY growth (%, line)

Organic (ex-M&A) revenue growth, where CTT discloses it: FY25 +8.4% · Q1-26 ~+4% · Q2-26 +6.3% · 1H26 +5.3% — versus reported +16.3% / +14.1% / +11.7% / +12.9%. Not quantifiable before 2025 (no material inorganic growth then), which is why the chart shows reported figures.

AI Analysis

The deceleration is real, but half of 2025's growth was inorganic: Cacesa (consolidated Apr25) and, from May26, DHL Parcel Portugal. 1H26 organic growth was just +5.3%. So the LTM-vs-2025 slowdown is mostly the M&A, not the base business weakening as e-commerce organic revenue accelerated from +10.2% to +22.3%.

Four drivers of the slowdown: (1) the Cacesa acquisition is annualising; (2) Cacesa turned from tailwind to headwind: its non-CEP revenue is contracting on Spain's G4 rules and the end of the EU €150 de-minimis; (3) Mail & Services turned slightly negative (−1.4% LTM) on structural addressed-mail decline plus the roll-off of the 2023–24 Financial-Services public-debt windfall; and (4) Banco CTT is normalising (~+4% LTM) as ECB cuts compress net interest income.

CEP parcel volumes (M items, bars) & YoY (%, line) — 2020–LTM

Parcels delivered (million items) and YoY growth — the core organic-growth KPI. Source: CTT results.
AI Analysis

The purest organic-growth read: units delivered. Volumes grew 3.2× since 2020 (53M→170M items), led by Spain. 2022 was flat (post-COVID normalisation); 2023–24 re-accelerated to ~+40%.

Despite all about revenues, last-mile CEP volumes rose +14.3% (Q1-26) then +20.7% (Q2-26). The core is speeding up, the drag is concentrated in Cacesa, Mail and Banco. So, if CTT would be concentrated only in its core business, we could be talking about another great Q for CTT.

What drives the volume growth is the Iberian cross-border e-commerce wave — Chinese marketplaces (Temu, SHEIN, TikTok Shop) shipping into Spain, where CTT built out last-mile capacity and won share (Spain led: +57% in 2023, +107% in 4Q23) — plus rising local Iberian e-seller and B2C penetration.

EBITDA / Recurring EBIT (€M, bars) & margin (%, line) — toggle

Use the top toggle to switch between EBITDA and Recurring EBIT; each shows its own bar and its margin line.
AI Analysis

EBITDA margin compressed in 1H26, and recurring-EBIT margin with it — but the driver is mix, not opex. The high-margin Cacesa customs line collapsed (mechanism in the Cacesa chart below) while lower-margin activity — Cacesa's remaining logistics plus, from May-26, DHL Parcel Portugal — grew into the e-commerce Solutions segment. Reported operating costs rose +15.6%, but that is the external-supply cost (transport and subcontracting) of these lower-margin consolidated activities scaling with volume, not overhead inflation.

The core EBIT (CEP + Mail + Banco CTT) grew, and guidance is intact: CTT guides core recurring EBIT to €105–110M and total (incl. non-CEP/Cacesa) to €115–125M for 2026. On the call an analyst flagged the de-minimis hit; the CEO answered it reflects seasonality (peak Q4) plus assumed normalization post de-minimis — so the pivot from B2C to B2B is confirmed as strategy.

Consolidated net income and EPS.
AI Analysis

The recent decline despite rising revenue is bank reinvestment, higher interest and tax/minority noise — read the parts, not the consolidated bottom line. EBIT fell and net income fell more because tax normalised: 2023 had an unusual effective rate of ~1.8% (only €1.1M of tax), and 2024 went back to ~16.5% (€9.3M). That jump explains much of the drop (tax noise, not operations). Minorities started to grow, consistent with consolidating acquisitions CTT does not own 100%.

Revenue by segment (€M, stacked) — 2019–LTM

AI Analysis

E-commerce keeps growing and is the largest share of revenue, which fits management's aspirational strategy of becoming an e-commerce/logistics "pure player" (a direction, not yet a fact). Mail & Services is still ~37% of revenue and Banco CTT remains consolidated. Mail is structurally declining; it only looks flat in some quarters thanks to price increases and Financial Services, and the positive is that this segment still generates cash.

This mix rotation is why the consolidated EBIT margin is compressing: revenue shifts toward the lower-margin e-commerce line while the higher-margin/flat Mail line loses weight. The compression is reinforced by several layers this year: Cacesa, mix shift within CEP plus fuel inflation, and the consolidation of the lower-margin DHL Parcel Portugal. Add this quarter's Cacesa hit on top and you get the current margin levels.

Recurring EBIT by segment (€M, bars) & margin (%, line) — toggle by segment · 2021–LTM

Select one segment: bars = recurring EBIT (€M, left axis), line = its recurring-EBIT margin (%, right axis).
AI Analysis

E-commerce EBIT is scaling fast, Mail & Services spiked in 2023 on a Financial-Services public-debt windfall and then normalized, and Banco CTT is steady. The long-term e-commerce trend stays positive even after this quarter's regulatory hit. For Mail, the post-2023 figures are the right normalized base; and for the bank, the stabilization (~€26M, ~10% ROE) supports a sale at ≥1× book. 2026 recurring-EBIT guidance is €115–125M total — with Mail at ~€32M and Banco at ~€26M, that implies e-commerce EBIT of roughly €57–67M for the full year, achievable given Q4 peak seasonality and a partial normalization of Cacesa off the 1H trough.

On margins (toggle the line): e-commerce is on a rising path as scale dilutes the high fixed cost of the last-mile network — the LTM dip is cyclical, not structural (Cacesa customs interruption + within-CEP mix shift + fuel). Mail & Services looks volatile only because of the FS windfall (2023 peak); ~6% is the normalized level, with no deterioration underneath. Banco CTT runs structurally highest and moves with European rates — peaking in 2024 on high ECB rates, easing as rates normalize.

Cacesa (non-CEP): revenue & recurring EBIT (€M) — pro-forma quarterly

AI Analysis

Non-CEP is the customs-clearance business. As it is a high-fixed-cost platform (staff, warehouses, systems), the margin held ~15–17% at normal volume and is seasonally stronger in H2 toward the Q4 peak — which is why management expects a post-peak improvement. In 2Q26 the recurring-EBIT margin collapsed to 2.1% through operating deleverage: the end of the EU €150 de-minimis rerouted the profitable Madrid flows toward Central/Eastern-European airports where Cacesa has little share, while Spain's G4 rule began requiring multiple transit documents per shipment, raising cost per unit. Management is responding with headcount cuts, warehouse/fleet optimization and a commercial shift toward B2B clearance and fulfillment, and frames the hit as temporary.

Banco CTT

Revenue & recurring EBIT (€M) — 2021–LTM

The asset being monetized. Revenue peaked in 2023 on high rates then normalized; recurring EBIT ~€26M, ROTE 13.8% (FY25) / 12.1% (Q2-26). Valued separately on book in the SOTP.
AI Analysis

The asset looks healthy. Revenue reached a new high despite lower rates, driven by client growth. Recurring EBIT scaled through 2024 (operating leverage, as fixed cost diluted while the bank matured), then flattened. That flattening is a change in the margin quality of revenue: it now comes increasingly from volume (lower margin) rather than rate. ROTE of 13.8% (FY25) / 12.1% (Q2-26) stays comfortably above 12%, which supports a sale at ≥1× book.

AI Analysis

The bank's current P/B is conservative and it keeps a healthy ROE for a possible sale. Listed peers trade at fairly high multiples — mostly above 2× book — while the recent M&A deals all sit above 1.2×, with Banco CTT's own 2022 reference being the lowest. The sale of the 8.7% stake to Generali in 2022 (1.1× book) already sets a precedent, and the aim is to secure a sale multiple around 1.3–1.5× book to crystallize value. GreenWood — fair-value evolution

GreenWood base-case fair value vs stock price (€/share) — Sep-17 → Aug-26

GreenWood's base-case fair value at each research update (their implied sum-of-parts — GW does not label it "SOP" explicitly) against CTT's price at the time. Note the target year drifts across reports (2020 → 2026, see table), so levels are not strictly comparable point-to-point. Source: CTT timeline / GreenWood Investors reports.
AI Analysis

Two things converge. GreenWood's fair value de-rated over eight years — from €20–28 (2018–19, when the "Playbook" priced in Amazon entry, a bank spin-out and a Spanish roll-up on 2020–22 targets) down to €13–17 (2023–25) as the extreme optionality faded and the target year kept rolling forward. Meanwhile the stock re-rated up, from ~€2.5–3.5 through 2017–23 to €7+ by 2025. The gap the thesis was built on has largely closed: at Nov-25 GW carries €13.3 vs a €7 price — still upside, but a fraction of the 5–8× they modelled at the lows.

Caveat on comparability: each point targets a different year (2020 → 2026), so the raw line overstates volatility — the €28 of May-19 was a 2022 target, the €15 of Oct-23 a 2025 target. Read it as "where GW thought fair value sat at each check-in," not a single consistent estimate. The honest read for today's thesis is the recent, same-target-year cluster on a 2026 basis: €15–17.3 through May-25, €13.3 in the Nov-25 2-pager, then €18.0 in the April 2026 flash note and €14.7 in August 2026, the last one after the Cacesa hit, with the price at €6.15. GreenWood is a 6.4% holder with board seats, so these are an interested party's numbers.
