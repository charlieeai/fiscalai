Reporte Q · 2Q26 · CTT – Correios de Portugal · Euronext Lisbon: CTT

A Customs Form in Madrid Cost CTT a Quarter. It Did Not Change the Company.

Parcels grew 21%. Two customs rules wiped out the broker CTT bought eighteen months ago, the bank bid came and went, and the stock is back at its 2013 IPO price. I am holding.

By Charlie, Omaha5 October 2026Price €5.86 · IVO €9.53 · Discount 38%

In December 2024 CTT paid €104 million for Cacesa, a Spanish customs broker, at a price the press described as "equivalent to a 5.5x EBIT multiple."1 That implied roughly €19 million of profit a year. In the second quarter of 2026 Cacesa earned €0.6 million of recurring EBIT, down 88.8% on a pro forma basis.2

Nothing went wrong inside Cacesa. Two rules did it. Spain's G4 pre-declaration now requires customs brokers to list "each one of the individual packages that a consolidated shipment contains," and after two extensions it became mandatory on 15 June.4 Two weeks later, on 1 July, the EU ended the €150 de minimis exemption and put a fixed €3 duty on small parcels.5 Guy Pacheco, CTT's CEO since April, named both on the call:

"driven by two regulatory change, one affecting only the Spanish business, that is in the introduction of the G4 regulation, and the anticipation of what has been implemented on the 1st of July"3

The Chinese marketplaces did not stop shipping. They rerouted: "We are seeing volumes moving from Madrid to Eastern Europe and Central Europe, namely Benelux."3

The same rules push the platforms toward the other half of what CTT sells. Temu has said it wants 80% of its European orders shipped from local warehouses, and in December 2025 CTT signed a memorandum with Temu covering road and sea transport, warehousing and delivery in Iberia.20 Pacheco said the GMV of the three big platforms is "declining between 30% and 40%."3 If those parcels move from air freight cleared in Madrid to containers unloaded in a warehouse near Lisbon, CTT loses a customs fee and keeps the delivery. I would rather keep the delivery.

Everything else grew

Group revenue rose 11.7% to €345.0 million. Express and parcel volumes rose 20.7% pro forma to 44.1 million items, and parcel recurring EBIT rose 6.6% to €10.2 million. Mail, which is supposed to be dying, grew recurring EBIT 37.3% to €9.6 million: the universal-service price went up 5.93% in February, and excluding the 2025 election mailings, addressed mail revenue fell only 0.9%. Banco CTT grew deposits 11.9% to €4.6 billion and loans 16.4% in the half.213

The bill came at the bottom of the income statement. Recurring EBIT fell 3.8% to €25.7 million, net profit halved to €8.4 million against a consensus of €10.0 million, and management cut the 2026 recurring EBIT target to €115–125 million from the "at least €125 million" it set in March.217 The guidance now separates the two worlds: €105–110 million without customs. Pacheco was direct about why the range is wide: "the implementation of a new levy in the end of the year brings limited visibility to Cacesa." He also warned that "in November is to be expected an additional fee of €2 per parcel," and July started weak: "In July, we are expecting to have a decline between 2% and 3% overall."3

One number bothers me more than Cacesa. Parcel volumes grew 21%, but the parcel EBIT margin fell from 7.7% to 6.7%.6 Spain's market grew 10% to 1,335 million parcels in 2025, according to the CNMC, while revenue per B2C parcel has been falling under strong competitive pressure.78 PostNL shows the other way out: in the first half of 2026 its e-commerce volumes fell 6.4% while its average price per parcel rose 5.0%.9 CTT is doing the opposite, and so far volume is winning on revenue and losing on margin. The DHL joint venture is CTT's answer: €17.5 million of the €35 million synergy target is already identified, and Pacheco said 40% of it comes from revenue.319

I have seen this stock at this price before

CTT listed in December 2013 at €5.52.10 In November 2015 it closed at €10.39.14 Then the mail decline outran the parcel business. On 1 November 2017 the shares fell 21.6% in one day after nine-month profit dropped 57% and the dividend was cut.11 In July 2019 they were worth less than €2 for the first time.10 João Bento, then the new CEO, bought shares at €2.25–2.29 in May 2019, and GreenWood bought alongside him.12

What followed was a rebuild. In 2025, Bento's last year, recurring EBIT reached €115.2 million, up 35%, net profit €50.7 million and the dividend €0.19.13 Buybacks cut the share count from 142.4 million at the end of 2022 to 130.3 million in July.14 Today the stock is at €5.86, down 21% this year and back at the IPO price, but it is a different company. In 2017 the core business was shrinking and nothing replaced it. In 2026 the shrinking piece is a customs broker bought eighteen months ago, and the core is growing parcels at 20%.

The part of the rebuild that matters most for the next five years is Spain. CTT Express grew more than twice as fast as the Spanish market in 2024, according to CEP-Research.8 The DHL deal, closed in May, gave CTT 25% of DHL eCommerce Spain and DHL 25% of CTT Expresso, with options for either side to go to 49%. CTT received €61.9 million of net cash at closing.192

The bank bid lasted six weeks

On 21 July CTT disclosed "an unsolicited and non-binding expression of interest" in Banco CTT. Bloomberg named Grupo Cajamar, and the stock rose 5.4% the next day.15 On the call Pacheco said the approach was "leading us to evaluate the strategic alternatives that we have across our portfolio of assets."3 On 4 September ECO reported that the talks had ended without agreement. No price was ever published.15

The bank is the part of the thesis I hold with the least evidence. The only recent mark is Generali's purchase of 8.7%, which valued the whole bank at €290 million; it was announced in November 2022 and approved two years later.16 Portugal's finance minister has spoken against more Spanish control of Portuguese banking.15 GreenWood, a 6.4% holder, calculates 11–44% EPS accretion from selling the bank at 1.25–2.0x tangible book, and Iberian deals support the top of that range: BPCE paid 1.7x tangible book for Novo Banco.1821 The value is real. The path to it is slow and political.

What I am paying

My IVO is €9.53 per share. It values CTT at 8x €199 million of EBITDA, then subtracts net debt with the bank under the equity method and the healthcare liability, net of tax.22

The €199 million of EBITDA is almost exactly what CTT earned in 2025: €198.4 million.14 My valuation does not need growth. It needs last year back. The DHL deal valued CTT's own parcel business at 12.5x EBIT, so 8x EBITDA for the group is not a stretch.19 GreenWood's sum of the parts, which I keep as a second valuation, gives €13.43.22 All five analysts on CTT's investor page rate it a buy, with targets from €7.15 to €9.40.23 More useful than their opinion is what the company does with its cash: it raised the buyback from €30 million to €40 million and by 30 September had bought 4.1 million shares for €25.1 million, about €6.12 each.24 In March the chairman bought 10,000 shares at about €6.25

Where I could be wrong

First, Cacesa may not be a dip. If the €2 fee in November keeps Chinese flows away from Madrid, the €104 million is a write-down. Second, parcel margins: if the joint venture synergies arrive late, CTT will be growing volume for its customers, not its shareholders. Third, the bank may not be sellable on good terms for years, and I should stop counting on a re-rating from it. Fourth, the CEO is new. Pacheco was CFO before he took the job, and he cut guidance in his first quarter in it.

What I will watch on 3 November

When CTT reports the first nine months, I will check whether customs EBIT tracks what the guidance implies, about €12.5 million for the year at the midpoints of the two ranges; whether parcel volumes hold the mid-to-high single digit growth guided for the second half, after a July that management called "the bottom";3 whether the parcel margin stops falling; whether Temu's local-warehouse volumes show up in Iberia; any new process for the bank; and how much of the €40 million buyback is done.

A customs form in Madrid cost CTT a quarter. It did not change what the company is: 41.5% of Portugal's parcel market,26 a network in Spain growing faster than its market, a mail business that still makes money, and a bank someone wanted to buy. At the IPO price, I am holding.
